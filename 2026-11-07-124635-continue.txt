● Yes, with one addition: the narrow walkways between seat rows are nodes too,
  not cells. Only the open areas use the 0.5 m × 0.5 m grid.

  ┌─────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┐
  │        Area         │ How it's represented │          Spacing           │
  ├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┤
  │ Hallways, foyer,    │ Grid cells of 0.5 m  │                            │
  │ main aisles,        │ × 0.5 m              │ Fixed at 0.5 m             │
  │ stairs, doors       │                      │                            │
  ├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┤
  │                     │ 1 node, 1,329 in     │ Wherever the seat actually │
  │ Each seat           │ total                │  is, about 0.5 m apart,    │
  │                     │                      │ with no snapping           │
  ├─────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┤
  │ Walkway between two │ 1 node per seat,     │                            │
  │  seat rows          │ directly in front of │ Follows the seats          │
  │                     │  it                  │                            │
  └─────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┘

  Why row walkways can't be cells: they're only 1.2–1.6 ft wide (about 0.37–0.49
  m), which is narrower than one cell. On the grid they would disappear or come
  out broken, so that's why the row areas are cut out of the grid and replaced
  with nodes.

  One row looks like this:

   seat ─ seat ─ seat ─ seat ─ seat      you can climb along seats (slow, cost 
  2.5)
    │      │      │      │      │        each seat steps out to its walkway node
   walk ─ walk ─ walk ─ walk ─ walk      walkway nodes chain along the row (cost
  1)
    │                            │
   aisle                       aisle     row ends link to the nearest 0.5 m cell
   cells                       cells

  A person's route out:
  1. They start on their seat node.
  2. They step onto the walkway node in front of them.
  3. They move along the walkway chain to the end of the row.
  4. They step onto an aisle cell, then move cell to cell through the aisles,
     doors and corridor.
  5. They leave through an exit, or balcony seats reach an exit by way of a
     one-way stair link to the first floor or a stage-side door.

  It's all one connected graph: a grid cell is treated like any other node,
  joined to its 8 neighbors. That means one distance calculation can cover the
  whole building, and a later simulation can walk it the same way everywhere.

  Row edges can differ slightly from 0.5 m: edges in the seat rows use their
  real lengths instead of exact cell steps. Seat to seat is about 0.5 m and seat
  to walkway is about 0.43 m. That's what decision 6 is asking you to confirm.

  I still haven't changed anything. Does this match what you had in mind? If so,
  you can reply "OK to all" or tell me which of the 8 decisions you'd change.

  - seats(): the packing search now steps the pitch down until a group has

    and row_regions().
  - row_regions() (new): each row's footprint plus its full walkway strip, so
    the lattice can cut both out.
  - _build_first_floor(): adds the walls that make the room 132 doors a true 3.5
    ft.

  grid.py
  - from_model:
    - takes cell_m and builds both levels in metres
    - cuts out the seating, then adds nodes, edges, exits and stair links
    - cell_ft and level="flat" now raise clear errors
  - _open_steps (new): tests every lattice step as a line against the geometry,
    so nobody passes through thin walls and diagonals can't cut corners.
  - _without_islands, _connected_labels, _row_link, _segments, _to_metres (new):
    remove unreachable fragments, find connected groups, link row ends, and
    handle geometry and units.
  - node_distance_field (new): searches backward from the exits over the
    directed edges, so one-way stairs stay one-way.
  - distance_field: the same search shown on the lattice. It has the blocked
    option, and targets now defaults to all exits.
  - exit_mask, door_mask (new): masks for closing a gate.
  - _node_mask, _lattice_values (new): convert between lattice arrays and node
    arrays. _node_mask rejects wrongly shaped masks with a clear error.
  - components: now labels nodes on the whole graph.
  - save, load: store the new fields, and load rejects old-format files with a
    clear error.
  - render: now shows both levels, labelled exits and stair heads.
  - xy, ij, cell, cell_m: now in metres.
