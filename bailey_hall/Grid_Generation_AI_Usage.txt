● It's in grid.py, in the _open_steps function at grid.py:121. The edges aren't
  actually removed, though: a step across a thin wall is never added in the
  first place.

  1. The test (grid.py:138-140): for each pair of neighbouring walkable cells,
     it draws a straight line between their centres. It then checks whether that
     line stays entirely on the floor:
  lines = _segments(center_x[rows, columns], center_y[rows, columns],
                    center_x[next_rows, next_columns], center_y[next_rows,
  next_columns])
  result[rows, columns] = shapely.covers(floor, lines)
     floor is the real building geometry, so it has the true wall shapes. A line
     that crosses a wall isn't fully on the floor, so that step comes back
     False.
  2. Diagonals (just below that, around lines 148-149): a diagonal step is only
     allowed if all four sides of its square are open, so people can't cut
     around the end of a wall.
  3. Building the edges (grid.py:366-376): from_model calls _open_steps, then
     adds lattice edges only for the steps that passed. Those steps are what end
     up in edge_from and edge_to.

  _without_islands (grid.py:154) uses the same check, so a pocket of cells
  closed off by thin walls counts as disconnected.

  This only runs while the grid is being built. After that, a wall exists only
  as a missing entry in the edge list, which is why my line drawing could
  recover the walls from the saved .npz.
